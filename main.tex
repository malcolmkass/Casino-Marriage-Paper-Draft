\begin{abstract}
IN PROGRESS, DO NOT CITE: Casino development is often promoted as an engine of local economic growth, but its broader social consequences remain less understood. This paper investigates how casino openings reshape demographic patterns and family-supporting institutions in U.S. counties from 2003 to 2023. Exploiting staggered treatment timing in a difference-in-differences framework, we document consistent evidence that casinos erode community structures. We estimate a 0.90 and 1.10 percentage-point decline in fertility rates for women and men, respectively, tied to a drop of 37 children per 1000 women and 45 per 1000 men. These effects result from recent movers to the community deciding to leave when a new casino opens. These demographic shifts are reflected in reduced school-age populations, especially in public schools, and in declines in religious adherence. The results demonstrate that casinos may generate social capital losses alongside changes in economic activity, altering the composition of local populations and undermining institutions historically central to family and community life.   
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\doublespacing
%Kass:  Global vs. Local effects of casinos

Local community officials often credit new casinos as significant economic drivers, which, in turn, promote economic inflows into the local area. \parencite{keranews2025irvingcasino} However, negative attention has come to light regarding the effects of gambling on local communities. While generating some economic benefits, often stemming from localized increases in employment and tax revenues, evidence is accumulating of the gambling industry's negative spillovers on the social strata. These effects are exacerbated due to the addictive nature of risky gambling behavior. For example, since the advent of online gambling, researchers estimate that only 4\% of individuals gain income from gambling and 5.3\% gamble more than 10\% of their monthly income. \parencite{Clark2024LegalizedGambling}  Gambling may also dampen local population growth, which is particularly concerning to officials. Communities may wish to promote inbound migration and fertility as sources of economic growth. Hence, researchers need to determine whether sin industries, such as gambling, generate these negative effects on community and family formation, including fertility. 





Our interests are two-fold. First, motivated by the current situation with a potential casino resort in close proximity to our academic institution, the University of Dallas, we focus on tabula rasa situations, where the first-mover casino resort enters a community without a prior casino or gambling presence in the immediate or surrounding vicinity. Hence, our questions of interest differ from research on "entertainment districts," where the economic theory may be less clear about the magnitude of possible outcomes.  Diminishing returns suggest larger effects for these first-mover casinos, for better or worse. Second, are the effects of casinos on family formation and fertility, as well as on community institutions, such as schools and religious institutions. Casinos represent a significant change to local communities, yet their effects on family formation and community institutions remain relatively unexplored. Current family formation research relies either on descriptive event analysis studies that focus on a single region or on studies that lack key covariates, which may limit coefficient precision. \parencite{moellman2013indian}  



This paper provides the first comprehensive analysis of how casino openings affect marriage, fertility, population composition, and family-supporting institutions using quasi-experimental variation from staggered casino openings across U.S. counties. We constructed a specialized dataset of casino openings across the United States matched to American Community Survey (ACS) microdata from 2000 to 2023 for family formation and education outcomes, and Religious Congregations \& Membership Studies data from 2000, 2010, and 2020 for religious adherence. We employ staggered Difference in Difference treatment timing to estimate causal effects. We find that the presence of a first-mover casino harms population growth trajectories in the local community relative to control areas without a casino, mainly by reducing the number of children.  We do not find changes in age-adjusted marital rates for women or men, but we do observe drops in external margin (percentages of families with children) and internal margin (numbers of children per family). The mechanism behind these responses is mainly driven by recent movers to the community ($\le$ 5 years).  Downstream effects of these demographic shifts include reduced school enrollment and reduced religious adherence and Catholic identity among the local population.

Second, the effects operate through selective out-migration of family-oriented residents, with long-term residents showing immediate fertility declines of 5.5-7.3\% at casino opening, suggesting that established families with more children leave casino counties. Third, casinos weaken family-supporting institutions, reducing school enrollment by up to 6.4\% and religious adherence by 35-37 adherents per 1,000 population in medium-religiosity counties.



\section{Background}



\subsection{Economic activity and Family and Community Formation Structures}

We define factors that contribute to family structures as marriage/cohabitation formations and fertility rates. Community structures are institutions that help build social capital, a key factor in helping families raise successful children, especially among lower-income families. \parencite{chetty2026opportunity} Development of social capital is tied to family participation in formal public and private education and religious institutions. 



Scholars identify several economic changes associated with new economic activity that may contribute to family formation and fertility.  These include incomes, property values, employment level, and wages. Looking at fertility through the lens of consumption, positive income shocks drive fertility, with findings ranging from North Dakota's Natural Gas boom  \parencite{kearney2018male} to commodity shocks driving fertility in oil-rich areas of Chile. \parencite{gallego2023baby} With this evidence, scholars speculate that the income effect of these shocks overwhelms a possible negative substitution effect due to an increase in the opportunity cost of fertility due to more advantageous economic opportunities. Viewing fertility as an investment, contrary to historical trends, increases the marginal propensity to save, which parents may view children as a way to smooth future consumption. \parencite{goldin2021career,doepke2023economics}  



\subsection{Casinos and Economic and Social Outcomes}



Casinos have significant effects on local communities, with uncertain impacts on family formation and community institutions.  Casinos have been found to increase tax revenues, employment, earnings, and property values. A number of papers find increases in local tax revenues and local economic activity. \parencite{humphreys2013new,wiley2011casino} However, many of these benefits found in the previous literature appear to be only a step-wise increase in growth, and with limited spillovers only to the hospitality and service industries.\footnote{The economic impacts from entertainment and tourism industries sharply diminish from their initial investment. Once the venue is established, it is difficult to expand gaming production with new capital investments, compared to manufacturing and other industries. Others find that casinos do not materialize in today's entertainment-saturated environment because casinos are unable to generate new spending in a community, as opposed to substituting for spending at other local entertainment venues. \parencite{Scavette2022Casinos,Krutz2022Casinos} Casinos have been found to increase employment, with mixed evidence on wages; some studies find employment gains without wage improvements. \parencite{cotti2008effect} Lastly, research has found that casinos can also increase property values for local retail businesses in their vicinity, particularly when located in a larger entertainment district. \parencite{wiley2011casino} Others find that these employment benefits, unsurprisingly, are centered in the hospitality and service industries. \parencite{humphreys2013new}.}



However, these benefits often don't outweigh the negative externalities associated with a casino's presence, such as increased crime. \parencite{Soriano2025Why, Scavette2022Casinos} Outside of some isolated cases of community welfare improvements in rural areas, \parencite{moellman2013indian}, there is an array of research findings that casinos generate negative externalities on local communities, primarily crime. The seminal paper in this regard is Grinols et al. (2006),  who find that casinos generate an 8.6\% increase in property crime and a 12.6\% increase in violent crime in casino location counties. \parencite{grinols2006casinos} These socially negative outcomes do not happen immediately, but after a lag of 3-4 years. Mechanisms involved include that casinos generate high concentrations of easy targets. Generally, people holding significant amounts of cash are located in concentrated areas. In addition, the income-reducing effects of casinos impoverish the lower-income segments of society, who may be drawn to crime. \parencite{Clark2024LegalizedGambling}  Outside of financial considerations, regarding the gaming activity itself,  gambling as a form of entertainment may cause a time substitution effect for parents, substituting away from family-supporting activities. Cohabitation, marriage, and family formation depend on available resources. This includes income, but also effort and time. These resources are strained due to the partner's absence from the household. Family partnerships are strong coordinating devices, reducing communication and search costs and mitigating financial risk by pooling resources. \parencite{kearney2023two} Hence, while decreases in incomes and crime are clear concerns, time substitution to gaming from child-rearing activities may compound and delay family formation. Lastly, some have concerns that, given the strong social norms tied to family and community formation, family- and community-supporting choices do not actually map to family- and community-preferred choices, highlighting that choices under strong social norms are often susceptible to the weak axiom of revealed-preference violations. \parencite{sen1973behaviour} However, evidence suggests that these behaviors correlate to stated preferences. \parencite{muller2022illusion} 



\section{Data and Summary Statistics}

\subsection{Dependent variables of interest: Demographics and Family formation}



Our analysis combines several datasets to examine casino effects across multiple domains. We use American Community Survey (ACS) microdata from 2000-2023 for family formation outcomes, Religious Congregations \& Membership Studies data from 2000, 2010, and 2020 for religious adherence. These publicly available datasets are merged with our key treatment variable, casino openings, using a privately constructed dataset, and then linked to government state and county FIPS codes. There were multiple sources for our treatment data, many of which involved scouring information, including World Casino News, which was the initial source. \parencite{WorldCasinoNews_CasinoOpenings} \footnote{\url{https://news.worldcasinodirectory.com/in-the-news/casino-news/casino-openings}} By compiling casino openings from 2003 to 2023, we then denoted the state and counties where these openings are located, as well as the adjacent counties to give our data a donut structure, where the treatment is the casino county and the control are counties considerably further way from these casino areas. The key time variable is the year of the opening. Using this data, we excluded areas with multiple casino openings during the 2003-2023 period, as well as casino- or adjacent-county areas with preexisting casinos before 2003. \footnote{As an example, Clark County in Nevada opened 16 casinos during that time frame} \footnote{This also includes adjacent counties to preexisting casinos}. Hence, only true tabla rasa casino openings are included in the treatment. Further-out counties without the presence of a casino or a pre-2003 casino are under control. In this 2003-2023 time span, there were 9 casino openings, 59 adjacent casino counties, and 388 control counties from 2003 to 2023.\footnote{Special thanks to Kim Burch, Grace Bason, and Luke Gonzales for their assistance in creating this dataset}



Table 1 presents summary statistics by county type. Notable differences emerge: casino counties have lower marriage rates (32\% vs 38\% in control counties), fewer children per person (0.707 vs 0.824), and different demographic composition with lower white representation (63.1\% vs 70.8\%), supporting the need for our difference-in-differences identification strategy.

\section{Empirical Strategy}



\subsection{Two-Way Fixed Effects}



We begin with a standard two-way fixed effects (TWFE) specification:

\begin{equation}
Y_{ct} = \alpha + \beta \cdot \text{Post}_{ct} + \mathbf{X}_{ct}'\gamma + \mu_c + \lambda_t + \varepsilon_{ct}
\end{equation}



\noindent where $Y_{ct}$ is the average number of children for women in county $c$ and year $t$, $\text{Post}_{ct}$ is an indicator equal to one if county $c$ has an operating casino in year $t$, $\mathbf{X}_{ct}$ includes demographic controls (average age and age squared), $\mu_c$ are county fixed effects, $\lambda_t$ are year fixed effects, and $\varepsilon_{ct}$ is the error term. We cluster standard errors at the county level and weight by population. The advantages of this method include tractability and the ability to control for time-invariant county-specific characteristics.

\subsection{Identification Strategy}



Our identification relies on the assumption that, conditional on controls and fixed effects, the timing of casino openings is quasi-random with respect to fertility trends. While casino placement is endogenous to economic conditions, we argue that the specific timing of openings—often determined by lengthy regulatory processes, ballot initiatives, and legal challenges—provides plausibly exogenous variation in treatment timing.



The key identifying assumption is parallel trends: absent casino openings, treated and control counties would have experienced similar fertility trajectories. We assess this assumption through event-study analyses and, in the future, examine its robustness to alternative control-group definitions and trend specifications.

\section{Moving forward}

\subsection{Callaway and Sant'Anna Method}

Given concerns about treatment effect heterogeneity and staggered treatment timing, we will implement the \parencite{callaway2021difference} approach. This method addresses potential bias in TWFE estimators when treatment effects vary across units or over time.

The CS estimator constructs group-time specific treatment effects $ATT(g,t)$ for cohort $g$ (year of first treatment) in period $t$:

\begin{equation}
ATT(g,t) = E[Y_t(g) - Y_t(0) | G = g]
\end{equation}

\noindent where $Y_t(g)$ is the potential outcome under treatment and $Y_t(0)$ is the potential outcome under control. The method aggregates these group-time effects into an overall average treatment effect on the treated (ATT):

\begin{equation}
ATT = \sum_{g} \sum_{t} w(g,t) \cdot ATT(g,t)
\end{equation}

\noindent where $w(g,t)$ are weights based on group sizes.

We will implement the doubly robust (DRIPW) estimator, which combines inverse probability weighting with outcome regression to improve efficiency and robustness. Counties that opened casinos before 2005 are excluded from the CS analysis as ``always treated'' units. However, we will not use this method in our main analysis as we believe the TWFE method is sufficient for our purposes.

\subsection{Robustness Checks}

To address potential confounding from county-specific trends, we will estimate specifications with county-specific linear time trends:

\begin{equation}
Y_{ct} = \alpha + \beta \cdot \text{Post}_{ct} + \mathbf{X}_{ct}'\gamma + \delta_c \cdot t + \mu_c + \lambda_t + \varepsilon_{ct}
\end{equation}

\noindent where $\delta_c \cdot t$ captures county-specific linear trends.

For the CS approach, we include county-specific trends as additional covariates in the outcome regression, allowing the method to condition on these trends when constructing treatment effect estimates.


\section{Conclusion}

This paper provides the first comprehensive evidence that casinos systematically disrupt family formation and weaken family-supporting institutions. The effects operate through both behavioral change among long-term residents and selective out-migration of family-oriented individuals. Our findings document previously unquantified social costs of casino development that should be considered in policy discussions about gambling expansion. These changes represent a fundamental shift in community character that persists and intensifies over our 8+ year observation window.
